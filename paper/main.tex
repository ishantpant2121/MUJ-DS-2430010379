\begin{abstract}
Health-insurance fraud detectors trained on known fraud categories can force novel schemes into familiar classes and confuse clinically rare but legitimate claims with fraud. We formulate \emph{explained-rarity filtering}: a claim is escalated only when it is unfamiliar and unexplained by clinical and provider evidence. We report three distinct synthetic experiments: an earlier tabular prototype; a standalone, independently trained LSTM, 1D-CNN and Transformer comparison over 36 model/fold/seed fits; and a temporal relation-summary pilot that implements additional OW-ERF stages. In the standalone comparison, the Transformer achieved mean known-class accuracy 0.809 and mean novel-mechanism AUROC 0.693 across the four held-out-mechanism folds and three seeds, while results were highly variable across folds. These experiments are not a single integrated HGT/R-GCN system and do not use real insurance claims. We report the methods, measured outcomes and remaining limitations without claiming real-world performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large claim volumes in health-insurance systems, including IRDAI-regulated insurance and the AB-PMJAY scheme in India, make exhaustive manual review impractical. Fraud mechanisms such as upcoding, unbundling, phantom billing and collusive referral rings also change over time. Three problems follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Closed-world classification.} A softmax classifier assigns a new scheme to the nearest known class, often with high confidence.
\item \textbf{Rarity is not fraud.} Pure anomaly scores flag legitimate severe cases because they are rare, complex and expensive.
\item \textbf{Alert fatigue.} Raw scores carry no statistical meaning, so a fixed threshold drifts and review budgets are exceeded.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A problem formulation, \emph{explained-rarity filtering}, that separates unfamiliarity from unexplainedness. (2) A pipeline (\ERF{}) combining post-hoc novelty fusion, split-conformal rejection and a heteroscedastic clinical expectation filter. (3) A benchmark protocol with a synthetic claim generator, leave-one-mechanism-out evaluation and leakage control. (4) Preliminary evidence from a tabular prototype, including where the filter hurts.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Open-set and open-world recognition.} Open-set recognition~\cite{scheirer2013,geng2021} and out-of-distribution (OOD) baselines~\cite{hendrycks2017,lee2018,liu2020energy} score unfamiliarity from logits or embeddings. On graphs, OpenWGL~\cite{wu2020openwgl} rejects unseen-class nodes with a variational graph autoencoder, and GNNSafe~\cite{wu2023gnnsafe} applies energy-based detection to graph neural networks.

\textbf{Graph fraud and anomaly detection.} CARE-GNN~\cite{dou2020care} counters camouflage with reinforcement-learned neighbor selection, and BWGNN~\cite{tang2022bwgnn} addresses heterophily. DOMINANT~\cite{ding2019dominant} and the PyGOD library~\cite{liu2024pygod} cover reconstruction-based detection, while BOND~\cite{liu2022bond} and GADBench~\cite{tang2023gadbench} provide benchmarks. These methods output novelty or anomaly scores and do not separate rare-but-clinically-explained claims from unexplained ones.

\textbf{Conformal prediction.} Split-conformal methods~\cite{vovk2005,lei2018,angelopoulos2023} give finite-sample marginal guarantees under exchangeability. Barber et al.~\cite{barber2023} study relaxations, and Huang et al.~\cite{huang2023cfgnn} apply conformal prediction to graph neural networks. Temporal claim streams violate exchangeability, so our guarantee is approximate and is tested empirically.

\textbf{Heteroscedastic regression.} Predicting a mean and a variance under a Gaussian likelihood~\cite{nix1994,kendall2017} lets the expected spread grow with severity.

\textbf{Healthcare fraud.} Prior work applies supervised and unsupervised learning to Medicare-style data~\cite{bauder2017}.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let a claim $v$ have features $x_v$, a graph neighborhood, and a label $y_v$ from benign, known mechanisms, or novel mechanisms. Training sees only benign and known mechanisms. At test time a claim is \emph{unfamiliar} if its conformal $p$-value satisfies $p_v \le \alpha$, and \emph{unexplained} if its clinical inconsistency ratio $a_v$ exceeds a calibrated threshold $\tau$. A claim is escalated if and only if it is both.

To prevent temporal leakage, the embedding of claim $v$ at adjudication time $t$ uses only the causal neighborhood
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}_r(v;t)=\{u:(u,v)\in E_r,\; t(u,v)\le t\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Encoder}
The target architecture uses a timestamped heterogeneous graph of claims, beneficiaries, hospitals, doctors, admissions, packages and diagnoses, with time-aware edges and an R-GCN or HGT encoder implemented using PyTorch Geometric~\cite{fey2019pyg,schlichtkrull2018,hu2020hgt}. Three complementary branches are separately implemented and trained in the released synthetic benchmark: an LSTM over recent claim-history sequences, a 1D-CNN over ordered procedure-code tokens, and a Transformer over relational-context tokens. The standalone experiment compares each architecture independently rather than claiming that it is already fused with a full HGT/R-GCN. The three models use different input modalities by design, so the comparison is a comparison of architecture-plus-modality pipelines. A separate relation-summary pilot implements causal relation-specific attention and additional OW-ERF stages, but it is not a full arbitrary-node heterogeneous graph encoder.

\subsection{Novelty score}
From classifier logits $z$, the energy is
\begin{equation}
E(x)=-T\log\sum_{k}\exp(z_k/T).
\end{equation}
The Mahalanobis score $M(h)$ is the minimum over known classes of the squared Mahalanobis distance of embedding $h$ to the class mean under the class covariance. With $F_E$ and $F_M$ the empirical CDFs of $E$ and $M$ on training data, the fused score is
\begin{equation}
u=\tfrac12 F_E(E)+\tfrac12 F_M(M).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conformal rejection}
On a calibration set disjoint from training and containing only known-class claims, with $n$ calibration scores $u_i$, the $p$-value is
\begin{equation}
p_v=\frac{1+\#\{i:u_i\ge u_v\}}{n+1},
\end{equation}
and a claim is rejected as unfamiliar when $p_v\le\alpha$ with $\alpha=0.05$.

\subsection{Explained-Rarity Filter}
A heteroscedastic model trained on benign claims only predicts a mean $\mu_j$ and standard deviation $\sigma_j$ for $j\in\{\text{log-amount},\text{length of stay}\}$ from \emph{exogenous} inputs (a severity proxy from prior history, comorbidity count, age) by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_g=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\Big[\ln\sigma_{ij}+\frac{(o_{ij}-\mu_{ij})^2}{2\sigma_{ij}^2}\Big].
\end{equation}
The inconsistency ratio is
\begin{equation}
a_v=\max_j\max\Big\{0,\frac{o_j-\mu_j}{\sigma_j}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
The threshold $\tau$ is set as a quantile of $a_v$ on benign calibration claims rather than by hand. The expectation model must not condition on fields a fraudster controls, such as the claimed diagnosis code; otherwise upcoding would make fraud look explained.

\subsection{Additional OW-ERF pilot components}
The separate relation-summary pilot implements episodic pseudo-novel energy-margin training, energy/Mahalanobis fusion, split-conformal rejection, legitimate-only clinical expectation and structural-reference thresholds, explained-rarity filtering, HDBSCAN grouping (with DBSCAN fallback when HDBSCAN is unavailable), cluster cards and audit-oriented metrics. It uses past-only entity summaries over six relation types, not a full PyG HGT/R-GCN over arbitrary typed nodes. The standalone LSTM/CNN/Transformer experiment is a separate benchmark and must not be reported as the full graph model.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\textbf{Data.} A synthetic generator produces benign claims, $5\%$ rare-severe legitimate claims, and four mechanisms: upcoding, unbundling, phantom billing, and collusion (represented in the prototype by two proxy features only). The fraud rate is $10\%$.

\textbf{Splits and metrics.} Three experiments are reported separately. The earlier tabular prototype uses five random seeds and its own i.i.d. split. The standalone deep comparison uses three generator seeds (42, 43, 44), four leave-one-mechanism-out folds, an internal known-only validation subset, a separate known-only calibration split, and an untouched test split, yielding 36 independent model fits. Known-class accuracy is calculated only on test claims from classes observed during training; novel-mechanism AUROC/AP treat the held-out mechanism as positive for novelty scoring. The v0.2 relation-summary pilot uses chronological train, selection, calibration and deployment windows. These results are synthetic, and the standalone benchmark does not establish real-world performance.

\textbf{Baseline.} Isolation Forest~\cite{liu2008iforest}, with its alert count matched to the number of \ERF{} escalations.

\section{Preliminary Results}
Table~\ref{tab:res} retains the earlier tabular-only reference results (means over five seeds). The conformal stage rejects between $4.3\%$ and $4.6\%$ of benign claims, close to the nominal $5\%$ on this i.i.d.\ synthetic split; this does not demonstrate validity under temporal drift. After the filter, benign escalation falls to about $0.5\%$. The filter cuts flags on rare-severe legitimate claims most clearly for phantom billing ($13.3\%\to2.6\%$). It also lowers recall: for collusion the drop is large ($0.586\to0.211$), because those claims look clinically plausible and would require graph structure that this prototype lacks. At a matched alert count, Isolation Forest recalls only $0.02$--$0.04$ of the three attribute-level mechanisms ($0.20$ for collusion) while flagging $22\%$--$28\%$ of rare-severe legitimate claims. \section{Limitations}
All measured experiments reported here use synthetic data. The independent LSTM/CNN/Transformer comparison reaches only moderate held-out-mechanism AUROC on average and shows high fold-to-fold variance; the 5\% conformal gate also has low recall for novel claims in this initial run. Known-class accuracy and novel-mechanism AUROC are different tasks and must not be collapsed into one accuracy claim. The standalone sequence models use generated proxies, and they are not integrated with a full graph encoder. The v0.2 relational pilot uses causal summaries of prior entity events, not an arbitrary-node PyG HGT/R-GCN; its package rates are parametric rather than curated from the official HBP 2.2 annex, and it does not ingest Synthea trajectories. The conformal guarantee is marginal and assumes exchangeability, which temporal streams violate, and it does not cover the explained-rarity filter. If severity evidence can be manipulated, fraud can be explained away. Only the declared small baseline set is currently run; full model baselines, ablations, repeated confirmatory runs and an audit of shortcut features remain necessary. Finally, wrongly escalated claims can delay care, so any real deployment requires human review and group-wise error analysis.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
The repository now contains an independently trained LSTM/CNN/Transformer synthetic benchmark, an earlier tabular OW-ERF prototype, and a temporal relation-summary pilot that implements additional open-world stages. The initial deep benchmark suggests that the Transformer is strongest on known-class classification, but novel-mechanism detection remains variable and weak at the configured conformal threshold. The next steps are to improve generator realism, complete Synthea/HBP grounding, implement a full temporal heterogeneous graph encoder, run the full baseline/ablation/seed plan, audit shortcuts and subgroup errors, and then evaluate on appropriately labelled real data.
